Suzuki has made a major move in next-generation EV technology by entering the solid-state battery race. The company announced an agreement to acquire the entire solid-state lithium-ion battery business of Japan-based **Kanadevia Corporation**, with closing set for **July 1, 2026**.

Osaka-based Kanadevia (formerly Hitachi Zosen) has been developing this technology since **2006**. Its **AS-LiB** solid-state batteries use dry-process solid materials instead of liquid electrolytes, reducing leakage risk, improving thermal tolerance, and enhancing safety. A notable proof point is space validation: these batteries were tested on the **external surface of the International Space Station** and certified by JAXA for space applications. The acquisition includes R&D assets, design know-how, and related sales activities.

Suzuki did not disclose financial terms and has not yet detailed its product roadmap. Still, this move positions the brand alongside Japanese rivals such as Toyota, Nissan, and Honda, as well as global groups including Mercedes, Stellantis, and Volkswagen in the solid-state race.
