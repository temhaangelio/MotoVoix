Yamaha Motor Corporation USA is leaving California, where it has operated its main base since 1979. The company announced that it will move its U.S. headquarters from Cypress, California to Kennesaw, Georgia. The transition starts in **late 2026** and is expected to be completed by **2028**.

Yamaha describes the decision as part of wider **structural reforms** aimed at improving profitability in its U.S. operations. It says tariff-related cost pressure reduced operating profit by **30.4%** in 2025. The **25-acre** Cypress campus is planned for sale, with a temporary sale-and-leaseback setup to support a smoother transition. The relocation directly impacts around **250 employees**.

This is not entirely a first move for Yamaha: the marine division moved to Georgia in 1999 and motorsports operations followed in 2019. Georgia already hosts more than **2,300 employees**. With this relocation, Yamaha plans to consolidate U.S. operations under one umbrella. Spokesperson Bob Starr summarized the decision clearly: **"Bringing all major units together in Georgia is a logical step to improve efficiency."**

Yamaha now joins a growing list of companies that have moved operations out of California in recent years.
